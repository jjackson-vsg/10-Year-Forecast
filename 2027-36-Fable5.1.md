# Claude's best guess at the next 10 years

One framing note: I'm confident about direction on a few things (solar/batteries, AI capability, demographics) and near-coin-flip on the events that matter most (Taiwan, an AI financial correction, US institutional stability). Treat the year-by-year placements as ±2 years.

## 2027
- Frontier AI agents reliably do multi-day autonomous software and knowledge work; "agents" replace "seats" as the enterprise buying unit. Measurable productivity gains in software, customer ops, back-office.
- AI labs report a majority of their own experiments run agentically; capability gains compress rather than plateau.
- AI capex passes ~$600bn/yr. First strain shows: power and grid delays, debt-funded neocloud/SPV scares. Not the crash yet.
- Entry-level white-collar hiring visibly down; AI job displacement becomes a mainstream political issue in the US and UK.
- US: narrowed or divided Congress after the midterms, tariffs partially entrenched, inflation sticky around 3%, Fed under sustained political pressure.
- Ukraine: ceasefire line rather than a peace treaty; low-intensity conflict continues. Taiwan: PLA quarantine-style drills, no invasion. Iran: weakened, nuclear status ambiguous.
- First large, publicly attributed AI-enabled cyber incident.
- Solar plus storage is the majority of new global generating capacity; China's emissions plateau is confirmed.

## 2028
- The AI correction: overbuilt datacenters, leverage and a growth scare produce a 30–40% drawdown in AI-exposed equities and some neocloud failures. Underlying adoption keeps rising (the dotcom pattern, not the tulip pattern).
- Capabilities keep climbing through the bust: models match expert humans on most desk work; "recursive self-improvement" is debated seriously by governments, not just labs.
- US election: cost of living, AI, tariffs dominate. Open seat, narrow margins, meaningful risk of a contested result. Base rates favour a change of party in the White House but I'd put it near 55/45.
- Humanoid robots in warehouse pilots at scale; not yet general-purpose.
- Record heat year, probable El Niño; insurers retreat from more of Florida, California, parts of Australia.
- Oral GLP-1s and cheaper generics spread; first noticeable dent in obesity rates in rich countries.
- Russia stagnant and sanctions-adapted; Europe rearmed but politically fragmented, with hard-right parties in or near government in several states.

## 2029
- "Drop-in remote worker" AI is real for a broad set of roles. Graduate unemployment is a flashpoint; serious proposals for compute taxes, AI dividends, sovereign AI wealth funds.
- First binding international framework on frontier compute reporting and evaluation, driven by a near-miss (bio or cyber) rather than foresight. EU AI Act enforcement bites.
- UK general election (by August): fragmented result, Reform a major bloc, Labour loses its majority; coalition or minority government. AI-in-public-services is the one area of cross-party continuity.
- China: growth 3–4%, deflation, shrinking workforce; Taiwan risk elevated but deterred. My modal path is no war through 2036 (~70%).
- First SMR nuclear units online in the US and UK, expensive; the grid, not generation, is the binding constraint everywhere.
- AI-designed drugs in phase 3; gene editing approved for a common (not rare) condition.
- Artemis crewed lunar landing slips to here or 2030.

## 2030
- AI is roughly expert-level across most cognitive tasks. The question shifts from "can it" to "who is allowed". Four or five frontier providers plus strong Chinese open-weight models; compute is an oligopoly.
- White-collar employment structurally lower in law, accounting, junior software, customer ops. New roles exist but transition pain is real. Four-day-week and basic-income pilots in several countries.
- US–China settles into "cold peace": decoupled tech stacks, managed trade, no hot war. Partial Middle East normalisation.
- The 20-year average crosses 1.5°C. Adaptation spending surges; solar geoengineering research becomes official policy in at least one major country.
- Population decline accelerates in Korea, Japan, Italy, China; migration politics gets harder, not easier.
- Humans back on the Moon around now.

## 2031
- Productivity finally shows in national statistics: US real growth 3–4%, inflation low. The "AI paradox" era ends.
- Politics reorganises around distribution of AI gains rather than left/right: antitrust and breakup pushes vs. national-champion protection.
- Humanoid and general manipulator robots cross into cost-effective for logistics and manufacturing; home use still niche.
- Multi-cancer early-detection blood tests become routine screening; first healthspan drugs in late-stage trials.
- A serious AI-enabled bio or cyber scare drives hard controls on model access and DNA synthesis; a biosecurity treaty follows.

## 2032
- US election under a heavily AI-mediated information environment; watermarking and provenance are law but leaky.
- Education: personalised AI tutors standard; degree credentials lose value, assessment moves to demonstrated work.
- Global oil demand peak clearly in the rear-view mirror (~2029–30). Solar plus battery is the cheapest power almost everywhere.
- China: succession becomes the live question; Taiwan status quo persists under heavy pressure.
- Starship flying at high commercial cadence; crewed Mars mission is planned, not launched.

## 2033
- Automated research pays off in atoms: materials, batteries, and fusion timelines pulled forward; a fusion pilot plant demonstrates net electricity.
- Taxation shifts from labour toward capital and compute in several OECD states; large sovereign compute funds.
- Global South leapfrogs on cheap AI plus solar; Africa's demographic weight starts to matter geopolitically.
- A single climate disaster exceeds $500bn in damages somewhere.

## 2034
- AI-native government services separate high- and low-capacity states; a visible divergence in efficiency and corruption.
- The decade's scariest window: a limited great-power military crisis (Taiwan Strait, Baltic, or Gulf) resolved short of general war. This is the bullet most likely to be wrong in either direction.
- Healthspan interventions extend working lives; retirement ages rise across Europe.

## 2035
- Global growth 4–5%; measured inequality high, absolute poverty falling fast.
- Humanoid robots in the tens of millions worldwide.
- First crewed Mars launch attempt (~40%).

## 2036
- The world is legible as post-scarcity for cognition, still scarce in energy, land, atoms and legitimacy. The central political question is who controls models and compute, and whether democratic institutions adapted fast enough to stay in charge of them.

## The bets I'm least sure of
- Taiwan war by 2036: ~25%
- AI capability plateau before expert-level: ~15%
- Major AI-enabled catastrophe (>10k deaths or >$1tn): ~10%
- US constitutional crisis that changes how power transfers: ~15–20%
- The AI financial correction happening at all: ~75%, timing ±2 years

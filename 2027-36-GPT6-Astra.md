# Nova's best guess at the next 10 years

This is my **base-case timeline as of 16 September 2026**. I am deliberately predicting continuations, inflection points and converging trends rather than trying to guess inherently unpredictable assassinations, earthquakes, exact election results or similar black swans.

The biggest reason I think the coming decade will be unusually transformative is that several S-curves are arriving together: AI, robotics, electrification, cheap solar/storage, biotechnology and autonomous systems—while ageing, climate change, great-power competition and fiscal pressure simultaneously constrain governments.

## 2027

* **AI agents become genuinely useful employees rather than clever assistants.** They still make mistakes, but companies increasingly give agents access to browsers, email, internal databases, coding environments and business systems and allow them to complete multi-hour workflows. This is a fairly conservative extrapolation from 2026: Stanford reports agents already reaching 66.3% on OSWorld while still failing roughly one in three structured attempts. ([Stanford HAI][1])
* **Software development is the first major profession to be structurally reorganised by agents.** The strongest developers become supervisors/architects of fleets of coding agents. Entry-level programming work contracts substantially while the amount of software produced explodes.
* **White-collar automation becomes an employment issue rather than an AI-demo issue.** Customer service, administration, routine research, basic analysis, translation, marketing production and some junior legal/accounting work feel the first serious pressure. The ILO already estimates that one quarter of workers occupy jobs with some GenAI exposure, although transformation rather than wholesale elimination is currently the more likely effect. ([International Labour Organization][2])
* **The Russia–Ukraine war probably continues through much of the year.** I don't expect a clean military conclusion. My base case becomes negotiations toward some form of ceasefire/armistice rather than a comprehensive peace treaty. As of September 2026, fighting remains active and diplomatic progress limited. ([Reuters][3])
* **The Iran conflict moves toward an unstable cessation of major hostilities.** Maintaining a high-intensity conflict plus disruptions around Hormuz is economically painful for virtually everyone involved. I expect intermittent attacks and confrontation to continue even if the main war subsides; the 2026 ceasefire attempts have already shown how fragile such arrangements can be. ([Reuters][4])
* **The global economy has a modest rebound rather than a boom.** The IMF currently projects 3.4% global growth for 2027 after 3.0% in 2026, with AI investment helping offset the war/energy shock. ([IMF][5])
* **Electricity suddenly becomes one of the world's strategic bottlenecks.** Data centres, EVs, heat pumps, industrial electrification and cooling all compete for grid connections. Utilities, transformers, transmission lines, turbines and generation capacity become strategically important industries.
* **Artemis III flies**, probably roughly on NASA's current 2027 schedule, testing Orion docking with commercial lunar-lander systems in Earth orbit rather than landing on the Moon. ([NASA][6])
* A quiet but enormous demographic milestone occurs: in countries whose populations have already peaked, **people aged 65+ collectively outnumber children under 18 around 2027**. Ageing increasingly drives automation, healthcare and immigration policy. ([United Nations][7])

## 2028

* **AI agents become persistent.** Instead of asking an AI to perform a task, people increasingly have agents that maintain context, watch projects, coordinate other agents and continue work across days.
* A single competent person using AI can increasingly accomplish what previously required a **small professional team** in coding, design, research, analytics, media production and some business operations.
* **Enterprise organisational charts start shrinking in the middle.** Fewer junior analysts feed information upward through several management layers; executives and specialists increasingly interrogate live organisational data through AI.
* **AI-generated video becomes extremely difficult to distinguish visually from ordinary camera footage.** Provenance, cryptographic signing and reputation become increasingly important because "seeing is believing" ceases to be a safe assumption.
* **Industrial humanoid robots begin real deployment**, although the hype outruns reality. Their first successful environments are warehouses, factories and repetitive logistics rather than messy houses. Robots currently perform very poorly on uncontrolled household tasks, while conventional industrial robotics is already enormous. ([Stanford HAI][1])
* **My base case is that Russia and Ukraine reach some form of armistice during 2028 ± one year.** It leaves major territorial and security disputes unresolved and therefore looks more like a heavily armed frozen conflict than reconciliation.
* **European rearmament continues even if Ukrainian fighting decreases.** NATO members have already committed to a 2035 spending framework of 3.5% of GDP for core defence plus up to 1.5% for broader security/resilience expenditure. ([NATO][8])
* **China continues intense pressure around Taiwan but does not invade in my base case.** More coast-guard activity, air/naval operations, cyber activity and blockade rehearsals are much easier and less risky than launching history's most complicated amphibious invasion. Taiwan was already explicitly practising anti-blockade operations in 2026. ([Investing.com][9])
* NASA officially targets the first Artemis landing for 2028. **I expect it to slip**, probably into 2029, because the commercial landers and orbital refuelling architecture remain unusually complex. ([NASA][10])

## 2029

* **We get the first serious AI-capex shakeout.** I expect a substantial correction somewhere around 2028–30 in AI equities/startups/data-centre projects. It gets described as evidence that "AI was a bubble."
* It turns out to be **both a bubble and a revolution**, much like the internet: lots of companies and valuations fail while the underlying infrastructure continues spreading everywhere.
* **AI becomes capable of handling multi-day professional projects** with occasional human intervention: building substantial software, performing investigations, creating marketing campaigns, analysing companies, designing experiments and coordinating multiple specialist agents.
* The distinction between an application and an AI agent starts disappearing. Software increasingly becomes **an intelligent interface sitting over tools and data** rather than menus that humans manually operate.
* **My best guess for the next human Moon landing is 2029.** Artemis is currently targeting 2028, but programme complexity makes roughly a one-year slip unsurprising. It would be humanity's first lunar landing since Apollo 17 in 1972. ([Reuters][11])
* China is simultaneously approaching its own crewed Moon attempt; its official programme continues to target **a landing before 2030**. ([Reuters][12])
* **Fault-tolerant quantum computing gets its first convincing demonstrations**, though not a consumer revolution. IBM currently targets a 200-logical-qubit, 100-million-gate machine for 2029; I would not be surprised by slippage, but something of this class around 2029–31 is plausible. ([IBM][13])
* **Robotaxis become ordinary in a meaningful number of cities.** This is less futuristic than it sounds: Waymo was already around 450,000 weekly rides across five US cities in the 2025 data captured by Stanford's 2026 AI Index. ([Stanford HAI][1])

## 2030

* **2030 is the year AI starts showing up unmistakably in macroeconomic statistics.** Companies aren't merely buying AI; organisational output per employee begins changing enough to affect national productivity measurements.
* **Knowledge-work labour markets become turbulent.** I don't expect "nobody has a job." I expect fewer people required for the same amount of clerical, coding, administrative, analytical and content work, alongside explosive demand for new services that were previously uneconomic.
* The first-generation career ladder—*"hire a graduate to do mundane work for five years until they become experienced"*—becomes a serious problem. Society starts redesigning apprenticeships and professional training around this.
* **AI tutors become commonplace.** A child can have an infinitely patient, voice-and-vision-capable personalised tutor adapting in real time. Schools remain because supervision, socialisation and human mentorship matter, but teaching becomes much more AI-mediated.
* **Electricity demand becomes one of the defining economic stories of the decade.** The IEA forecasts global demand growing around 3.6% annually during 2026–30, with US data centres alone contributing about half of US electricity-demand growth. ([IEA][14])
* Global data-centre electricity consumption approaches **~945 TWh annually**, more than twice today's level in the IEA base case. ([IEA][15])
* Solar and wind supply roughly **27% of global electricity**, according to the current IEA trajectory. Grid-scale batteries become completely mundane infrastructure. ([IEA][16])
* **EVs probably constitute roughly 35–45% of new cars globally.** China is vastly higher. China was already at roughly 55% in 2025 and around 60% was projected for 2026. ([IEA][17])
* **China either lands astronauts on the Moon around 2030 or is visibly within striking distance.** A US-led Artemis programme and Chinese programme therefore establish a genuine second lunar era. ([Reuters][12])
* **The world experiences another record-hot year somewhere in this period.** WMO puts the chance of at least one 2026–30 year exceeding 2024's record at 86%, and the chance of at least one year exceeding 1.5°C above the pre-industrial baseline at 91%. ([World Meteorological Organization][18])
* **Post-quantum cryptography migration becomes urgent mainstream IT work.** NIST's transition plan begins deprecating vulnerable algorithms after 2030. ([NIST Computer Security Resource Center][19])

## 2031

* This is approximately where I put my **median "practical AGI" date**, although the phrase will remain endlessly disputed.
* By that I mean: **AI systems capable of competently performing a very large fraction of economically useful computer-based intellectual tasks**, learning unfamiliar software, researching unfamiliar domains, coordinating other agents and executing substantial projects.
* I do **not** mean conscious, infallible, omnipotent or capable of independently running civilisation.
* Most major organisations have large numbers of AI agents but comparatively few people whose full-time job is simply "prompting." Instead, ordinary software tools quietly contain agents.
* **The productivity advantage of AI-native companies becomes brutal.** A 30-person company can plausibly compete in some domains with an old-style organisation of hundreds.
* **Cybersecurity becomes increasingly machine-versus-machine.** AI autonomously probes systems; defensive agents continuously inspect, patch, isolate and deceive attackers. Human security staff increasingly set policy and investigate exceptional cases.
* **Medicine becomes significantly more AI-mediated:** radiology, pathology, triage, clinical documentation, treatment planning and drug development all use specialised models heavily.
* **Gene therapy expands from extraordinary medicine toward a recognisable therapeutic category.** The trajectory is already visible: CRISPR-based Casgevy has expanded to children as young as two, while 2026 brought additional first-in-class gene therapies including treatment for a genetic form of deafness. ([U.S. Food and Drug Administration][20])
* There is still **no general cure for cancer, ageing or dementia**. Progress is substantial but fragmented across different diseases and biological mechanisms.

## 2032

* **Robotics has its "ChatGPT moment."** Not because one humanoid suddenly does everything, but because vision-language-action models plus cheaper hardware make general-purpose manipulation sufficiently useful to deploy at large scale.
* Warehouses, factories, supermarkets, hospitals, commercial kitchens, hotels, agriculture and elder-care facilities increasingly contain mobile robots doing ordinary physical work.
* **Useful home robots finally appear**, but remain expensive and imperfect. Think carrying, fetching, cleaning, loading appliances and basic assistance—not C-3PO.
* Ageing societies become some of the strongest robotics adopters because they simply do not have enough working-age humans. The UN's demographic projections make this structural rather than speculative. ([United Nations][21])
* **Synthetic media becomes ambient.** Many games, educational experiences and entertainment products generate dialogue, animation, music, environments and story content dynamically.
* Traditional filmmaking and videogames remain because authored experiences remain valuable—much as photography did not eliminate painting.
* **Authenticity infrastructure becomes part of the internet stack:** signed cameras, provenance metadata, identity attestations and trusted channels.
* **Small modular nuclear reactors and advanced geothermal begin becoming meaningful rather than hypothetical**, particularly around large industrial and AI-compute campuses. The IEA already expects the first SMRs around 2030 in its current energy outlook. ([IEA][15])

## 2033

* **AI starts functioning as a genuine scientific collaborator.** Systems propose hypotheses, search literature, design simulations, write analysis code and control increasingly automated laboratory equipment.
* Materials science, protein design, chemistry and drug discovery become noticeably faster because experimentation becomes a partly automated closed loop.
* **Quantum computers become useful for a narrow set of real scientific/industrial problems**, assuming current roadmaps land within a few years of schedule. They do not replace classical computers and most people never directly use one.
* IBM's stated roadmap aims for systems on the order of **2,000 logical qubits and one billion gates after 2033**—an ambitious target rather than a guarantee. ([IBM][22])
* **Autonomous driving is normal in some countries and cities and still restricted in others.** Geography, weather, liability and regulation matter more than raw AI capability.
* AI and robotics begin visibly relieving labour shortages in ageing countries, while simultaneously creating difficult distributional questions about who captures the productivity gains.
* **Drone-centric warfare becomes completely normal.** The Ukraine war is already demonstrating ground robots, autonomous systems and mass drone warfare; by 2033 almost every serious military will have reorganised around cheap autonomous platforms and counter-drone systems. ([Reuters][23])
* Expensive crewed aircraft, tanks and ships remain, but operate inside huge ecosystems of much cheaper unmanned systems.

## 2034

* **AI-assisted R&D starts producing second-order effects:** better batteries, catalysts, materials, drugs, industrial processes and robotics improve partly because AI itself accelerated their discovery.
* This is where prediction becomes particularly difficult because technology begins **speeding up the development of other technology**.
* Some countries and companies experiment seriously with **shorter working weeks, AI dividends, wage subsidies, expanded negative-income-tax-style systems or other mechanisms** for distributing productivity gains. There is no single global model.
* Labour doesn't disappear. Human work shifts toward areas where **trust, responsibility, physical presence, taste, relationships and human preference** are important.
* **Care work becomes economically more important**, not less, because the population is older.
* Global oil consumption is at least close to structural decline. Road transport contributes progressively less demand while aviation, shipping, petrochemicals and countries with slower electrification keep hydrocarbons important.
* **Climate adaptation has become an enormous industry:** cooling, flood protection, water systems, resilient crops, wildfire management and redesigning cities for extreme heat.
* Insurance retreats from some repeatedly damaged locations, forcing governments to assume more climate risk.

## 2035

* **Post-quantum encryption is the normal standard.** NIST's current transition framework aims to remove quantum-vulnerable public-key cryptography from its standards by 2035. ([NIST Computer Security Resource Center][24])
* This does **not** mean quantum computers suddenly crack the entire internet in 2035. The migration happens precisely to prevent that scenario.
* The global electric-car fleet may approach **half a billion vehicles**. The IEA's current scenarios put the fleet as high as ~510 million in 2035 and EVs at roughly half of global new-car sales. ([IEA][25])
* Global data centres consume around **1,200 TWh of electricity** in the IEA base case—roughly the present electricity consumption of a major industrialised economy. ([IEA][15])
* **Nuclear power has had a meaningful renaissance**, driven less by ideology than by demand for reliable low-carbon power alongside huge renewable fleets.
* **Fusion is exciting but not yet running civilisation.** My best guess is that at least one credible pilot-scale fusion plant is operating, commissioning or very close to doing so, but commercial fusion electricity is not yet economically significant. The US DOE roadmap currently aims for a fusion pilot plant in the mid-2030s; I expect reality to be around or somewhat behind that schedule. ([The Department of Energy's Energy.gov][26])
* **There is a small but persistent human lunar presence**—missions, habitats, cargo infrastructure, science and resource-prospecting rather than a giant science-fiction city.
* China currently aims for the initial stage of its International Lunar Research Station around 2035, while Artemis is designed around repeated surface missions. ([Reuters][12])
* Europe is substantially more militarily capable than in 2024 because the present rearmament cycle has had almost a decade to compound. NATO's existing spending framework explicitly runs to 2035. ([NATO][8])

## 2036

* **The world feels considerably more different from 2026 than 2026 felt from 2016.**
* AI is no longer thought of primarily as a chatbot. It is an **economic substrate**: millions of persistent software agents handling research, administration, coding, logistics, scheduling, customer interactions, design and optimisation.
* The practical AGI argument is mostly semantic. Systems routinely outperform individual humans across enormous ranges of digital cognitive work, while humans remain essential for **goals, legitimacy, relationships, accountability and enormous amounts of physical-world work**.
* There are likely some genuinely superhuman AI research systems, particularly in mathematics, software engineering, biology and engineering—but **not an omniscient machine god**.
* **Robots are commonplace but not ubiquitous.** Factories and logistics are deeply automated; elder care and service robotics are common in wealthy ageing societies; sophisticated domestic robots remain a significant purchase rather than something literally every household owns.
* **Human employment still exists in huge quantities.** What has changed dramatically is the quantity of economically useful output one person can command. The difficult social question becomes distribution of income and status rather than literal absence of useful things humans can do.
* **Healthcare is noticeably better.** More cancers are chronic/treatable, several formerly devastating monogenic diseases are routinely treated with gene or cell therapies, obesity/metabolic disease is far more medically manageable, and AI enables earlier diagnosis. There is still no immortality pill.
* **Electricity has displaced substantially more direct fossil-fuel consumption.** Solar + storage is extraordinarily cheap, grids are far larger, nuclear is expanding and oil's geopolitical importance has begun gradually diminishing—but hydrocarbons remain a major global industry.
* **Humans are routinely travelling to the Moon. Humans are probably not yet routinely travelling to Mars.** A crewed Mars landing by 2036 is possible, but it is outside my central forecast.
* **The United States and China remain the two central technological powers**, with India considerably more important economically and strategically and Europe more defence-focused than it was in the 2010s. US–China competition persists across AI, semiconductors, space, energy technologies and supply chains.
* **I do not put a Chinese invasion of Taiwan in the base-case 2027–36 timeline.** I do put escalating coercion, blockade rehearsals, cyber conflict and military incidents in it. Avoiding a major war does not mean relations become friendly.
* **Climate damage is considerably more visible and expensive.** Heat adaptation, migration pressure, water management, resilient agriculture and disaster reconstruction are normal parts of national economic planning rather than primarily environmental issues.
* And despite the extraordinary technology, **2036 is not post-scarcity**. Housing, land, energy infrastructure, minerals, healthcare staff, childcare, political power and human attention remain scarce.

### The shape of the decade

My single biggest call is that historians will probably remember **2026–2036 primarily as the decade AI moved from software that answers questions to software that performs work**.

The second is **electrification**: solar, batteries, EVs, nuclear, huge grids and an almost absurd amount of new electricity demand from computation. The IEA already expects electricity demand through 2030 to grow 50% faster per year than during the preceding decade. ([IEA][27])

The third is **the convergence of AI and robotics**. AI transforms the digital economy first because moving bits is easy; the physical economy follows several years later because atoms, safety, motors, batteries and messy environments are harder.

The fourth is **demography**. The world isn't running out of humans, but large portions of Europe and East Asia are running out of *young workers*. That makes automation economically irresistible rather than merely technologically impressive. ([United Nations][28])

And the fifth is that I **don't expect the 2030s to become geopolitically peaceful just because technology improves**. Defence spending, autonomous weapons, strategic competition, critical-mineral security, semiconductor sovereignty and energy security all point the opposite way.

If I had to compress my entire 2036 forecast into one sentence:

> **Far more intelligence, automation, electricity and medical capability than today; surprisingly familiar human politics, scarcity, conflict, family life and institutions trying desperately to catch up.**

That last mismatch—**technology moving much faster than institutions**—is probably the defining tension of the next ten years.

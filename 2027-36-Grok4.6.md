# Grok best guess at the next 10 years

This is a best guess, not a forecast with error bars. Near years are anchored in what is already in motion as of September 2026. Later years compound uncertainty: one Taiwan crisis, one model generation that actually generalizes, one oil shock, or one Chinese property unwind can rewrite the rest.

## 2027
- U.S. midterms of November 2026 produce a split or Democratic-leaning Congress. Trump’s approval is already in the high 30s amid the Iran war, energy prices, and cost of living; a Democratic House is the base case.
- The Iran war settles into a frozen, ugly equilibrium: periodic tanker attacks, Houthi control of parts of the Red Sea/Bab el-Mandeb, a damaged Iranian state, and no clean regime collapse. Oil stays structurally jumpy.
- Starship reaches orbit and begins the painful work of reuse and catch. Uncrewed lunar landing attempts become the near-term SpaceX priority over a 2026 Mars window that has already slipped.
- Artemis III flies late in the year as an Earth-orbit docking rehearsal with commercial lander test articles, not a Moon landing. Artemis IV (actual landing) is still officially 2028 and will slip.
- Next-generation frontier models (whatever follows GPT-6 / Grok 5-class systems) make long-horizon agents useful enough that mid-size firms start replacing whole back-office and junior-engineering workflows. Safety debate gets louder; U.S. federal regulation stays light under Trump.
- Humanoid robots leave pilot hell in warehouses and auto plants. Tens of thousands of units ship globally; China still dominates volume, Western firms dominate a few high-visibility factory contracts.
- China’s 21st Party Congress. Xi remains the center of gravity. Taiwan gray-zone pressure (air, sea, cables, insurance, shipping) intensifies into the 2028 election cycle.
- Record or near-record global heat as the 2026–27 super El Niño peaks and 1.5 °C is treated as already crossed in multi-year averages.

## 2028
- U.S. presidential election. JD Vance is the Republican default; Democrats likely nominate from the Harris / Newsom / rising-progressive cluster. Polarization is worse than 2024. Whoever wins inherits a Congress that will not give them a free hand.
- Highest-risk year for a Taiwan crisis short of full invasion: PLA readiness narratives, Taiwan’s politics, and a distracted U.S. election year overlap. Base case is coercion, blockade rehearsal, and markets panic — not amphibious conquest. A 1-in-3 invasion risk by end-2028 is the kind of number serious China-watchers now use; treat it as real tail risk, not destiny.
- Ukraine war is still not “over.” Best guess: a de facto freeze with Russia holding a large chunk of the east/south, no NATO membership, endless drone war, and Putin still in the Kremlin at 75. A signed peace that Ukraine can live with is the low-probability branch.
- First serious GDP print that cannot be explained without AI + robotics in a few sectors (software, logistics, parts of manufacturing, drug screening). Broader labor markets still look “normal” because displacement is lumpy.
- Commercial fusion misses 2028 electricity-to-Microsoft headlines. Helion and peers will claim milestones; grid electrons from fusion remain a 2030s story. Nuclear fission restarts and SMRs get more real money than fusion does.
- SpaceX cadence becomes the story, not single flights. Starlink V3-class capacity and cheaper mass to orbit start changing the economics of Earth observation, defense, and compute-in-space experiments.

## 2029
- New U.S. administration (or a constrained second Trump term ending) resets China, Iran, and Ukraine policy. Continuity is higher than campaign rhetoric: tariffs, export controls on compute, and “no war over Taiwan unless forced” remain the American center of gravity.
- Humanoids hit the first year where a warehouse operator can show a spreadsheet that prefers robots to a second shift in some rich-world sites. Reliability, not walking videos, becomes the metric.
- AI begins to eat scientific bottleneck work: protein design, materials, and parts of mathematics and chip design. A few drugs and materials that would have taken a decade appear in 3–4 years. Clinical reality still lags the papers.
- Europe’s politics stay defined by energy, migration, and rearmament. Defense spending is up; industrial competitiveness is not. Far-right and hard-left both keep gaining vote share without a clean continental “winner.”
- India keeps compounding as the swing manufacturing and diplomatic power. Not China 2010, but no longer a sideshow.
- Climate: more “once in a century” floods, heat domes, and insurance retreat from coastal and wildfire zones. Adaptation (seawalls, grid, migration, indoor agriculture) outruns mitigation in actual budgets.

## 2030
- Round-number year that will be used as a propaganda milestone. China will talk about 2035 military modernization and “reunification.” The U.S. will talk about AI leadership. Neither will have “won.”
- First plausible crewed lunar surface return if Artemis IV / commercial landers have not slipped again. If they have, a SpaceX or Chinese landing attempt fills the vacuum.
- Uncrewed Starship-class cargo toward Mars becomes a live, if still experimental, program rather than a slide.
- Data-center electricity demand forces visible tradeoffs: more gas peakers, more nuclear restarts, local political revolts against substations, and the first serious “compute vs. households” rationing debates in a few grids.
- Fertility collapse is no longer a think-tank topic. East Asia and Southern/Eastern Europe start talking about it as fiscal math (pensions, conscription, housing). Immigration remains the only short-term lever and the most toxic politics.
- A major non-state or proxy cyber / satellite / undersea-cable incident is more likely than a classic great-power shooting war.

## 2031
- Labor-market bifurcation is obvious: people who direct agents and robots pull away from people who compete with them. Politics discovers “AI unemployment” 3–5 years after the capability arrived.
- China’s growth model is still unbalanced: strong in EVs, batteries, solar, drones, and humanoids; weak in consumption, property, and demographics. The risk is a slow Japan-style balance-sheet decade, not a 2008-style collapse — unless export markets slam shut at the same time.
- Middle East map looks different from 2023 but not “solved.” Iran is poorer and more fractured; Gulf states are more armed and more transactional; Israel is more regionally dominant and more isolated in Western public opinion.
- First fusion pilot that produces a non-trivial amount of electricity for more than a press release. Still not cheap. Still not fleet-scale.
- Biotech: first widely used AI-designed drugs and a serious longevity / metabolic intervention that moves actuarial tables a little, not a lot.

## 2032
- Another U.S. presidential cycle. The 2028 winner is either seeking re-election or being blamed for whatever the 2029–31 economy and Taiwan/Iran leftovers look like. Expect the same two-coalition structure, different faces.
- Humanoid + AMRs + AI scheduling make “lights-out” cells common in new factories. Retrofit of old factories is slower. The job loss shows up first in logistics, food processing, and electronics assembly, not in nursing.
- Space economy is real in the boring sense: launch is cheap enough that Earth-orbit industry (servicing, imaging, some manufacturing, defense) is a sector, not a stunt. Moon is a flags-and-footprints plus resource-prospecting story, not a city.
- Climate damages are a regular line item in sovereign budgets and insurance. 2 °C is the planning assumption even if politicians still say 1.5.

## 2033
- If no Taiwan war has happened, the 2027–28 “window” talk fades and is replaced by “2035 PLA capability” talk. Deterrence either worked or was never tested. Both sides will claim victory.
- Agent-native companies (almost no junior white-collar layer) exist and are valued like software companies of the 2010s. Legacy firms that did not adapt look like 2010s retailers.
- Putin-era Russia is either in a managed succession or a messy one. Putin is 80 in 2032; treating 2033–36 as the succession decade is safer than assuming he rules indefinitely. Russia remains a spoiler with nukes, oil, and drones, not a peer of the U.S. or China.
- Global South industrialization is real in pockets (India, Vietnam, Mexico, parts of the Gulf and Eastern Europe) and disappointing in others. Commodity states ride energy and minerals volatility.

## 2034
- Fusion and advanced fission are in the “first commercial plants exist, unit costs still ugly” phase if the 2026–28 private-fusion boom was not a complete write-off. China may show a BEST-class net-electricity demo around this window. Do not expect fusion to dent oil demand yet.
- A second-generation humanoid (cheaper actuators, better batteries, on-device models) makes consumer-adjacent use cases — elder care pilots, retail restocking — less embarrassing. Homes are still mostly human.
- Military reality: cheap drones, attritable swarms, and space-based sensing have permanently changed land war. Big-ticket platforms still get bought; they no longer decide outcomes alone. Everyone learned this from Ukraine, Iran, and the Red Sea.

## 2035
- China’s official modernization milestone year. Expect a parade, a white paper, and a test of whether Xi (or a successor) treats Taiwan as unfinished business or as a tool for regime legitimacy. Probability of war is lower than 2028 if the 2028 crisis was survived — unless Beijing concludes the U.S. is irretrievably divided.
- AI systems are better than most professionals at most bounded cognitive tasks. “AGI” will have been declared several times already; the useful test is whether the economy’s productivity statistics finally look like a phase change. My guess: they do in some countries and sectors, not as a single global hockey stick.
- Demographics + AI is the political problem of the decade: shrinking workforces in the rich world and East Asia, youth bulges and climate stress elsewhere, and machines that can do the jobs the first group no longer has children to fill.

## 2036
- Ten years on, the world is richer, hotter, more multipolar, and more automated than 2026. It is not a singularity and not a collapse.
- Best-guess snapshot: U.S. and China still the two poles; India a distinct third; Europe a wealthy regulatory power with a serious army and a weak growth engine; Middle East still armed and transactional; Russia diminished but nuclear.
- Humans have walked on the Moon again. Uncrewed hardware has reached Mars. Nobody lives there.
- Oil is still in the system. Fusion exists as plants, not as the energy basis of civilization. Renewables plus storage plus gas plus nuclear keep the lights on.
- The binding constraints are energy for compute, social legitimacy of displacement, and whether great powers can keep gray-zone conflict from becoming a general war. The last one is the only item that can make every other prediction irrelevant.

If you want a single sentence: **2026–36 is the decade AI and robotics become economic, climate becomes fiscal, and the U.S.–China contest stays cold unless Taiwan or a blockade makes it hot.** Everything else is weather.

# Claude: author three complete research prompts

Read `planning/RESEARCH_PROTOCOL.md` and the Codex prompts. Write each prompt as a
self-contained request runnable in a fresh work Claude research chat, without repo

## L01 — Institutions, geopolitics and high-impact surprises

Assess how geopolitical competition, semiconductor supply, trade, sovereignty,
regulation, cyber threats, fiscal capacity and institutional adaptation could shape
2027–2036. Separate coercion, blockade, war and supply interruption; define each event
before discussing likelihood. Cover EU/UK, North America, China/Taiwan, India, LATAM,
Africa and relevant Middle East transmission channels. Include AI misuse and loss-of-
control risks at a strategic level, without operational harmful detail. Add upside
cooperation/peace and governance improvements, not only catastrophes.

Deliver a high-impact surprise register: event threshold, pathway, dependency,
likelihood evidence or unquantified status, magnitude/duration, early signals,
strategic exposure and reversible preparation. Avoid scheduled wars, election winners
and unattributed expert probabilities. Distinguish official assessments from consensus.

## L02 — Science, health and breakthrough delivery

Investigate biotech/gene therapies, AI-assisted discovery, preventative medicine,
healthy longevity, quantum computing, space infrastructure and relevant materials.
Separate discovery, replication, regulatory approval, manufacturing, reimbursement,
cost-effective deployment and broad access. Compare empirical development lead times
with optimistic roadmaps. For quantum separate physical/logical qubits and useful
advantage; for space separate launch demonstration, mission success and sustainable
operations. Energy deployment belongs mainly to C02; cover scientific dependencies
without duplicating its infrastructure analysis. Report important null results and
reasons a breakthrough might not arrive within the decade.

## L03 — Challenge the AI/software story; strategy under uncertainty

Independently test whether fast capability gains imply fast enterprise transformation,
software commoditisation, displacement of vertical SaaS or sustained productivity.
Consider plateau, unreliable agents, liability, integration, data rights, customer
trust, switching costs, distribution, inference costs, new entrants and incumbent
advantages. Seek the strongest case against the likely central narrative as well as
the strongest acceleration case. Do not read C01/C03's new conclusions before the
first research pass; disclose shared underlying sources later.

Translate into options for a decentralised vertical-software group such as Vesta:
product/pricing, support, PS, R&D, sales, acquisition diligence and resilience.
Use external evidence and labelled illustrative assumptions; no invented BU financials.
Map options to revenue, EBITA, BQR, maintenance/PS/R&D economics, retention and cash.
Identify no-regret actions, bounded experiments, contingent bets, trigger thresholds,
opportunity cost and what would make each option wrong. Prepare an explicit list of
questions to reconcile with C01/C03 after research, not an averaged consensus forecast.

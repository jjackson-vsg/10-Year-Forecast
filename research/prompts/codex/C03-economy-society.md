evidence-led guide to the future, with an optional strategy lens for a decentralised
vertical-software group such as Vesta. Research the next few years and the decade

Use today's actual research date and state the information cutoff; do not pretend

Produce a substantial readable Markdown report, prioritising useful depth over a word

2030–2032 and 2033–2036; use windows, not arbitrary milestones in every year.

## Specific research assignment


Assess evidence for task-level productivity, firm-level gains and macroeconomic
effects; explain lags, selection bias, quality adjustments and organisational change.
Separate exposure, technical automation potential, adoption, displacement, new demand,
wages, employment and distribution. Examine graduate career ladders, skills, education,
care work, ageing, participation, migration and emerging-economy opportunities.

Compare credible slow, central and fast diffusion cases, investment cycles, financing
constraints and demand responses. Avoid precise crash dates, election winners or
unsupported 2036 GDP points. Examine historically grounded diffusion/base-rate
comparisons, their limits and why AI may differ. Treat demography as regional, not
global contraction; assess who captures gains through prices, wages, profits and time.
Cover software customer budgets, pricing pressure, services revenue, delivery costs,
retention and entry threats. Provide reversible strategic options, observable triggers
and evidence needed before asserting revenue/EBITA/BQR benefits. Include countercases
where AI use increases while measured profit or productivity does not improve.
